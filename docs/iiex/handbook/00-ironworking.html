Ironworking Expanded is the iron tier: everything you need to smelt iron in bulk before you own a single boiler. It is deliberately self-sufficient - the whole chain runs off an axle and a waterwheel, and nothing in it needs steam.

The loop. Crushed iron ore and lime flux go into a Burdenmaker, which drops them together into burden - the ore-bearing charge. Burden and coke are then charged into the Cold Blast Furnace through a Tall Hopper, as alternating layers rather than as a mixture. The furnace is blown with cold air from a Twin-Tub Blower and renders the charge down to molten pig iron and slag. The pig runs out of a tap into a molten canal, which plumbs it to wherever you want to cast it.

Pig iron is not a finished metal. It is hard, brittle and useless for tools - it is the shared intermediate everything downstream is made from. Remelt it in a Cupola Furnace - pig and scrap iron go straight in, layered with coke, no preparation needed - and you get cast iron, which casts into machine parts and substitutes for vanilla iron in build recipes. Work it down instead and you get wrought iron, which is vanilla iron. Steel comes later, and needs the steam tier.

What the iron tier leaves behind. Plated pipe - the first of three pipe tiers - and the molten-canal network, both of which every later tier builds on. Cast iron tools and parts. And slag, which is not waste: it lays as paths, slabs and stairs.

Where to start. Build the burdenmaker first - nothing else works without burden, and it asks for nothing but fired brick and no power at all. Then the furnace, then a blower on an axle. The canal can wait: pig solidifies at the tap and can be chiselled out by hand until you have somewhere better to send it.
