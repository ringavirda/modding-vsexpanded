Molten metal is plumbed, not carried. You never pick liquid iron up. It leaves a furnace tap into a molten canal and flows, cell by cell, to wherever you built the canal to go.

Every cell owns its own metal. A canal is not a pipe with one shared pool - each block holds what is standing in it, and each tick some of it moves to the next cell along. That is why a long canal takes visible time to deliver, and why a canal left standing full slowly goes solid: metal cools where it sits. A solidified cell is not lost, but you will be chiselling it out.

The pieces:
- Canal Start - sits under a furnace tap and takes the runout
- Canal (straight, bend, T-junction, cross) - the run itself
- Canal Tap - drains the run out; park a barrel under it to fill
- Mold Pedestal - pours into a mold set on it, which is how you cast
- Molten Barrel - a standalone vessel for holding a heat

Building a run. Every canal shape can be cut from cobblestone, from coloured running brick, or from fire brick - hammered and chiselled over fire clay. The rock or brick you use is cosmetic; pick whichever you have. A T-junction forks a heat between two stations, which is how one tap feeds a mold and a barrel at once.

Taps are shut by default. A closed tap severs the run at that point, so metal backs up behind it instead of draining. Open it with rightmouse when you actually want the run to empty.

Casting. Set a mold on a Mold Pedestal and the pedestal pours into it from the canal it is fed by. Cast iron parts, ingots and tool blanks all come out this way. What you can cast depends on what is in the canal - pig from the blast furnace, cast iron from the cupola.

Slope is not modelled. Metal moves along connectors, not downhill, so a canal can run level. What it cannot do is cross a gap: every cell must touch the next.
