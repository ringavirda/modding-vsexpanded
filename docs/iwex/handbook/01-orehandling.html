The furnace does not eat ore. It eats burden - a blended charge of crushed iron ore, crushed coke and lime flux. Burden cannot be crafted by hand; it comes out of an Ore Mixer, and preparing it well is most of what decides how your furnace runs.

The Ore Mixer is a megablock driven by an axle. Load it with the three components and it blends them into burden, stamping the mix it made onto the output: how much iron, how much flux, how much fuel. That stamp travels with the item, survives being split and stacked, and is what the furnace reads when you charge it. Block-info names the grade it produced and flags a charge that is off-spec or short of flux.

Coke is the number that matters. A coke-rich burden burns hot and blows easily, but eats fuel and drinks air. A coke-lean burden saves fuel - and packs so dense that the blast can barely get through it, so it demands far more pressure than a hand-driven blower can raise. At the iron tier you run rich burden and accept the fuel cost; running lean is what the steam tier is for.

Blocks required:
- 1 x Ore Mixer (axle-driven)
- 1 x Ore Bunker (any burned-brick colour)
- 1 x Tall Hopper, over the furnace shaft

The Ore Bunker is the buffer between them: burden in, burden out, one heat's worth at a time. Its front opening accepts a vanilla chute or Archimedes screw, so a mixer, a bunker and a hopper can be strung together to charge the furnace on their own while you do something else.

Coke, not coal. Coke is baked from coal in a coke oven; raw coal in the mixer makes a charge the furnace will grade as off-spec. Crush the ore and the coke in a pulverizer. Even scrap iron bits crush back into crushed iron and go round again.

One pile, one melt. The mixer packs burden dense - a single full pile holds most of a heat, so you are not shuttling stacks all day.
