Steelmaking Expanded adds a full industrial pipeline for turning out iron and steel in bulk, and it works in two stages. In Stage I you build a Blast Furnace and the support machinery around it to smelt iron, and in Stage II you bolt a Bessemer Converter onto that line to refine the molten iron into steel. Build the iron stage first and get it running reliably, because the steel stage is expensive and feeds directly from it. Be warned that the blast furnace is built from tier-3 refractory bricks, so you must already have reached the steel age to attempt it, and none of this machinery runs without steam power - have a steam setup working before you start.

The full chain is spread across four companion articles. The Blast Furnace is the heart of the operation and where Stage I begins; Hot Blast & Gas Handling covers the cowper stoves, the smoke stack and how everything ties into the pipe network, without which the furnace will never reach iron's melting point; Molten Metal & Casting explains the canals, taps and molds that turn the melt into finished items and where to route surplus metal or waste slag; and The Bessemer Converter opens Stage II, the heavy machinery that blows iron into steel and draws on every mechanic before it. The pipes, boilers and engines that power all of this come from the companion mod - see Steam Power - and are used both to supply mechanical power and to pressurise the air the furnace breathes. The furnace itself is an Ironworking Expanded shaft furnace at heart: the burden, the charge piles and the molten canals all come from that mod, and its Ironworking articles cover them in depth.

In brief, a full iron run goes like this. You prepare burden - crushed iron ore fluxed with lime - at an Ironworking Expanded Burdenmaker, and bake coke from coal in a coke oven. The furnace takes the two as alternating layers, nothing premixed: you load the Reinforced Hopper with coke and let the Bell Hopper beneath it lay the layer down the shaft, then load burden, and so on in rounds until the shaft is full. Lay coke first, because the furnace lights itself the moment an unbroken course of fuel covers the bottom of the shaft - there is no door and no torch. A powered Air Blower on a steam engine pushes air through a heated Cowper Stove and in through the Tuyeres as hot pressurised blast, while the Smoke Stack vents the excess gases. You hold the furnace past iron's melting point of about 1482 °C in the melting phase, alternating between two cowper stoves so the incoming air never goes cold, and once iron and slag have pooled you open the Molten Metal Taps to drain them down the canals into molds or barrels before they have a chance to solidify. Keep the rounds coming, the exhaust flowing and the boilers boiling, and repeat.
