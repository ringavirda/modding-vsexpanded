# Pig iron — pig, chunk, bit

**Status** live - the three items, the helve-break, its conservation test and the settled 375 u mass all
ship (2026-08-05), and the casting bed produces pigs in survival   **Mod** iiex
(`IronIndustryExpanded`)

**Owns** - the facts this page is canonical for:

* the three denominations `iiex:pig` / `iiex:pigchunk` / `iiex:pigbit`, their exact ratios, their stack
  sizes and the fact that they are one family authored in one file;
* the divisibility contract - the complete list of numbers in `src/` defined as a multiple of
  `ItemPig.PigUnits` (§ Numbers);
* the helve-break on the item side: `iiex:pigworkitem-iron`, the `iwexpigbreak` marker, the
  `iwexpigbreakrem` sub-bit remainder, the 6 × 2 × 5 anvil fill, the 10-voxel smithing target and the
  Harmony payout that pays the shed voxels out;
* the family's brittleness declarations - `workableTemperature: 0`, `meltingPoint: 1150`, and why the pig
  is `IAnvilWorkable` but never forgeable;
* the family's assets: the one authored shape and the two borrowed ones, and the drift between the
  runtime pig and its editable source;
* the family's `scrap`-role grant (`materialroles.json:8-10`), which makes the pig, the chunk and the
  bit cupola remelt feedstock.

## Role

Pig iron is the shared intermediate: the one thing a blast furnace makes and the one thing every
downstream iron process starts from. On its own it is a dead end - brittle, unforgeable, and worth
nothing until it is put through a second machine (`assets/iiex/lang/en.json:39`, `:357`).

The family exists in three sizes because a bed pour never lands on a whole number of pigs. The casting
bed freezes whatever units reached a mold, and a runner's stranded charge is a random amount; without a
smaller denomination the remainder is either destroyed (violating R2) or rounded up (minting metal).
Chunks and bits keep the harvest arithmetic exact (`BlockEntitySandCastingBed.cs:416-424`).

A pig cannot be smithed but it can be shattered, because it is brittle: it is the one metal in the game
that is workable at any temperature and yields nothing but smaller pieces of itself
(`ItemPig.cs:95-96`, `ItemPigWorkItem.cs:15-17`). The helve-break turns a bulk casting into carriable
scrap without a furnace, a hammer skill or a heat.

`workableTemperature: 0` is the whole brittleness model. It is vanilla's own soft-lead trick, honoured by
`ItemWorkItem.CanWork`, and it is what lets a pig be set on an anvil cold
(`ItemPigWorkItem.cs:15-17`, `ItemPig.cs:101-102`). The declared `MaterialDensity` differs between the pig
(7200) and its own work item (7000), and both differ from the metal descriptor's 7000
(`assets/iiex/config/metals/pigiron.json`) - none of the three is the unit rule, see
[density rule § Gotchas 1](../mechanics/density-rule.md).

### The denomination chain

| Constant | Value | file:line | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ItemPig.PigUnits` | 375 | `Items/ItemPig.cs:39` | one cast pig |

| `PigBreaking.UnitsPerVoxel` | derived, `PigUnits / PigVoxels` = 2.5 | `Items/PigBreaking.cs:36-37` | the only expression of the density rule in code - value and derivation are [density rule](../mechanics/density-rule.md)'s |

1 pig = 15 chunks = 75 bits.

### The divisibility contract

Everything below is defined as a multiple of `ItemPig.PigUnits`. This is the exhaustive list - a
repo-wide grep for `PigUnits` outside `ItemPig.cs` and `PigBreaking.cs` returns exactly these:

| Site | Expression | file:line | Owner of the number it produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| slag brick mass | `SlagBrickUnits = ItemPig.PigUnits` | `Items/SlagItemDefinitions.cs:27` | this page (it is the pig) |
| carved mold capacity | `ImpressionsPerMold(row) × ItemPig.PigUnits` | `BlockStructures/Casting/SandBedLayout.cs:167` | [casting bed](../machines/casting-bed.md) |
| harvest denomination | `units / PigUnits`, then `% PigUnits / ChunkUnits`, then `/ BitUnits` | `…/BlockEntities/BlockEntitySandCastingBed.cs` (`Denominate`) | this page |
| slag-brick denomination | `units / SlagBrickUnits` | same file | [casting bed](../machines/casting-bed.md) |
| HUD "ready" count | `cell.CellAmount / ItemPig.PigUnits` | same file | [casting bed](../machines/casting-bed.md) |
| handbook voxel count | `PigBreaking.PigVoxels` | `Items/ItemPig.cs:135` | this page |

The pig and the slag brick share one cavity by construction. The bed cuts one impression shape and pours
whatever reaches it, so giving the two products different masses would strand a remainder in every slag
pour and make a bed's yield depend on which liquid arrived (`SlagItemDefinitions.cs:21-26`).

### The 375 and the density rule

Settled 2026-08-05 and shipped: `PigUnits` and `PigVoxels` move together, so `UnitsPerVoxel` stays
2.5 and an anvil voxel of pig iron means the same 2.5 u as a voxel anywhere else in the mod - bed
cavity, crucible band, cupola band, anvil. The derivation is [density rule](../mechanics/density-rule.md)'s.

The denominations divide exactly at 375: 15 whole chunks, 75 whole bits, `SlagBrickUnits` follows by
definition, and the bed's mold capacities are `ImpressionsPerMold`-derived rather than literal
(`BlockSandCastingBed.cs:43-47`). The anvil fill is 5 × 3 × 10 = 150 voxels, positioned to cover the
smithing recipe's 10-voxel target (`ItemPig.cs:141-148`).

### The helve-break arithmetic

| Step | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| place | `ItemPig.TryPlaceOn` (`:104-125`) | refuses if the anvil already holds a work item (`:107-108`); resolves `iiex:pigworkitem-iron`; copies the pig's temperature (`:115-119`); sets the `iwexpigbreak` marker (`:121`); fills the voxels (`:123`) |
| fill | `ItemPig.CreatePigVoxels` (`:141-148`) | a solid 5 × 3 × 10 = 150 block at `x 4…8, y 0…2, z 6…15`, material `1` (Metal) |
| target | `PigRecipeDefinitions.cs` | a single-layer `["#####", "#####"]` pattern - 10 voxels, worth one chunk |
| hammer | vanilla `BlockEntityAnvil.OnHelveHammerHit`, wrapped | prefix snapshots the metal-voxel count, postfix diffs it (`AnvilPigBreakingPatches.cs`) |
| pay out | `PigBreaking.Emit` (`:45-55`) | adds `removed × UnitsPerVoxel` to the carried remainder, pays whole chunks then whole bits, keeps the sub-bit crumb |
| carry | `AnvilPigBreakingPatches.cs` | the crumb lives on the work item as `iwexpigbreakrem` (the marker key + `"rem"`) |
| spawn | `AnvilPigBreakingPatches.cs` | an item entity at `Pos + (0.5, 1.0, 0.5)`, carrying the work item's temperature |

A full break is 140 shed voxels (fourteen chunks) plus the recipe's own 10-voxel leftover (one chunk):
fifteen chunks, 375 u, exactly the pig (`PigBreaking.cs:13-15`, pinned by
`test/IronIndustryExpanded.Tests/Items/PigBreakingTests.cs`).

`GetHelveWorkableMode` returns `FullyWorkable` in two places - on the pig itself (`ItemPig.cs:130-133`)
and again on the work item (`ItemPigWorkItem.cs:47-50`) - because the base `ItemWorkItem` only helve-works
the plate and blister-steel recipes.

### Where a caller hooks in

* To make pigs, hand units to `BlockEntitySandCastingBed.Denominate` - it is `static` and pure,
  so a second caster needs no bed.
* To consume a pig, match the code path `"pig"` the way the puddling hearth does
  (`BlockPuddlingHearth.cs:115`). There is no `IsPig` helper and no material role for the family.
* To break something else on the helve, give it `IAnvilWorkable`, a marker attribute of its own and a
  target recipe; the payout maths in `PigBreaking` is independent of the item (`PigBreaking.cs:7-9`).

## Gotchas

1. The 150 voxels are an anvil footprint, not the drawn shape. `PigVoxels` is chosen so the fill covers
   the recipe target and `UnitsPerVoxel` stays 2.5; the drawn item is a different solid, and the volume
   audit is [density rule](../mechanics/density-rule.md)'s.

2. The marker key is used twice, with a suffix. `iwexpigbreak` is the boolean flag on the work item
   (`PigBreaking.cs:32`); `iwexpigbreakrem` is the float remainder
   (`AnvilPigBreakingPatches.cs`). Renaming `MarkerKey` silently moves both, and an in-flight break loses
   its crumb.

3. The patch counts voxels over the whole `[16, 6, 16]` grid, material `1` only
   (`AnvilPigBreakingPatches.cs`). Slag voxels (material `2`) are not counted, so a work item that
   somehow acquired slag would under-report and pay out less - safe (no minting) but not conserving.

4. The final hit pays nothing. When the helve completes the recipe, `WorkItemStack` is already null in
   the postfix and the method returns early. That is correct - the recipe's own `pigchunk` output is the
   last 10 voxels' worth - but it means the payout and the recipe output are two different mechanisms
   that must stay in sync by hand.

5. `ItemPig.GetMatchingRecipes` returns every smithing recipe the pig satisfies
   (`ItemPig.cs:96-99`), not just the pig one. Today only `PigRecipeDefinitions` names `iiex:pig` as an
   ingredient, so the list has one entry; a mod adding a second pig recipe would silently offer it on the
   anvil.

6. A chunk is 25 u and so is nothing else. `BfUnitsPerSolidNugget` is 5 - the same number as `BitUnits`
   - but they are unrelated constants in unrelated files (`IiexConfig.cs` vs `ItemPig.cs:41`), and the
   extinguish residue drops `game:metalbit-iron`, not `iiex:pigbit`
   (see [ironmaking](../processes/ironmaking.md)).

7. `ItemPig` is `[ItemRegister] partial` (`:27-28`) - the attribute generator supplies the other half.
   Adding a hand-written `Definitions` overload to the same class collides with the generated one.

## Open

1. The art disagrees with itself and with the mass. The runtime shape is two elements, the working-tree
   editable source is one, and the shipped mass implies 150 vx³ - a third volume again (§ Assets). One of
   the three has to move, and the editable file is uncommitted so the change is not even recorded yet.

2. There is no `IsPig` predicate. Every consumer string-matches the code path
   (`BlockPuddlingHearth.cs:115`, `:183`). The [burden](burden.md) family has `Burden.Is` / `Burden.IsCode`
   for exactly this reason; the pig family has nothing equivalent, so a third consumer will invent a
   fourth spelling.

3. The direct-charging route bypasses the pig entirely. The settled design routes molten pig straight from
   the furnace to a converter, demoting the beds to overflow, but the iron line still needs solid pigs
   (puddling takes nine, the cupola remelts them), so the family never becomes optional. Nothing in `src/`
   distinguishes a converter destination; see [cold blast furnace § Open 4](../machines/blast-furnace-cold.md).

4. No handbook page for the family. `assets/iiex/config/handbook/` covers ironworking, ore handling, the
   cold blast furnace, molten canals and the design table. The pig is described only in the ironworking
   overview text (`assets/iiex/lang/en.json`).
