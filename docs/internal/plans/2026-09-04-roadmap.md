## The goal, restated

Two content mods on one framework — `iiex` and `siex` on `exlib` — that put a 19th-century ferrous
industry into Vintage Story as something the player *runs*: ore into a blast furnace, pig into castings
and puddled bars, bars through a rolling mill and a machine shop into the parts of a steam plant, then
steel, alloys and high pressure on top of it. The pillars are fixed in [overview.md](../../design/overview.md):
build complexity buys operating efficiency; every upgrade removes a named friction; nothing is hidden;
historically grounded; extensible by other modders. `iiex` alone must be a complete experience (the
low-tech stop), a playtest group has been waiting for it since 2026-07-30, and the release target
([scope.md](../../design/scope.md)) is the whole ferrous line.

Two consequences drive the order below:

1. **A walkable `iiex` beats breadth.** One more designed machine adds nothing a playtester can touch;
   one craftable roll set closes a loop. Everything siex-only queues behind the iiex survival loop.
2. **Footprint-changing art lands before the first release.** `iiex`/`siex` are new mods, so nothing
   published is at risk today; the moment they ship, a grown engine footprint or a moved port breaks
   somebody's save. The polished steam art therefore goes in before the release candidate, not after.

## Assessment

**What is right and should not be touched.** The thesis is coherent and rare — the whole tree is
denominated in one currency, operating friction, and every machine either creates it or buys it down.
The furnace models (heat balance, raceway carbon, breach and choke as opposites, blow-in as a stored
bit), the pipe and molten networks, the rolling two-round model with friction-walked gaps, the die and
roll-set idiom where tooling carries its job, and the gear-count spine of the machining line all reinforce
each other rather than compete. The verification culture is unusual for a mod: a parity oracle, mutation-
checked guards, and a harness that stands machines on real footprints. The August art pass raised the bar
again — sourced proportions, vanilla's own gauge vocabulary, modelled bores and readouts animated from
state — and it was done by the owner, so it is not going to be argued back down.

**Where the risk is.**

1. **The feel is unverified.** Everything from U4.4 to the boiler megablock — the hearth, taps and
   plug, puddling, reheat, mill, shear, benches, rack, workbench, coke oven, crucible furnace, boiler —
   has passed its tests and been seen by nobody. The mod's whole value is what those verbs feel like,
   and pacing numbers (stroke cooldowns, soak rates, coke cycle, bridge torque) are all marked "tune in
   play". This is the cheapest highest-value work in the repo and it is a week of the owner's time, not
   code.
2. **The survival loop does not close.** Roll sets are lathe-turned by ruling and there is no lathe, so
   the forming shop cannot be reached in survival; the cast pipe segments have no recipe, so the Bessemer
   cannot be raised in survival either. Both close on the same unit — the machining line — which by the
   owner's own reading is mostly built already (one blocktype with shape/cell/job variants; only
   hold-to-operate, the shared window, seven footprints, seven exports and the job tables remain).
3. **Breadth is the standing temptation.** Forty-five machine pages, twenty of them designed and unbuilt.
   The owner's rulings already say it — content waits on infrastructure, the machine count is the
   budget — so the plan adds no machine that a loop does not need, and parks the rest by name.
4. **The steam art overhaul is a lot of code.** Twelve or so blocks, several new (horizontal engine,
   condenser, injector, indicator, tiered MP pump and blower), a footprint change on the engine that is
   there today, and a ruled model change (engines become flow-through). It is also the owner's freshest
   investment and it must land before the first release, so it gets its own phase rather than being
   sprinkled.
5. **Two legibility decisions to confirm before they harden.** Indicator blocks make plain pipes stop
   showing state — right historically, but it takes away the readout a player debugs a network with; and
   the station windows on six of eight machine tools are where "you work the machine, not a menu" is
   most at stake. Hold-to-operate is what keeps them honest; it must ship with them, not after.
6. **`siex`'s design debt is real but not release-critical for `iiex`.** The gas system has four
   recorded gaps, D4 and the ladle sizing are open, hadfield does not exist in code, N1 is contradicted by
   shipped data. Deciding these now would cost the iron loop weeks; they are Phase 5's first task.

### Phase 1 — walk the iron loop in game *(M; owner in the client, assistant fixing)*

A survival-and-creative walkthrough script, one section per unwalked station, each with the exact
gestures, the readouts to expect and the known unknowns to look at (the boiler's `waterRendererBox`, the
rack renderer, the coke cycle length, the hearth seating). Order: blast furnace with the new taps and
plug and blow-in → puddling heat → helve → reheat → mill → shear → benches → rack → workbench → coke oven
→ crucible furnace → boiler megablock (its CB gate list). Output is a defect list with a fix per item, and
the first real pacing numbers. Nothing new is built in this phase; it is the cheapest way to find out
whether the last five weeks feel right.

### Phase 2 — the machining line: the keystone *(L)*

Plan of record to be written from [machining-line.md](../../design/mechanics/machining-line.md),
[machines.txt](../workbench/machines.txt) and the owner's 2026-08-20 insight (one block, two families).

1. **Hold-to-operate** in exlib — `ProcessJob.Seconds` consumed only while RMB is held, built once for
   the six stations that want it and the workbench's craft sequence.
2. **The station family** — generalise `BlockShear`/`BlockEntityShear` into a `machine-tool` blocktype
   with a `type` variant; the shared station window extracted from `GuiDialogDesignTable` into exlib;
   seven footprints from machines.txt (the legend is redefined per machine); seven shape exports; the
   nail cutter and riveter fold into the family.
3. **Job tables** — the minimum roster that closes the loop: roll sets from cast roller blanks (lathe),
   cast pipe-parts into the four cast segments (bore, closes B19), the gear chain (shaper; bevel via
   lathe; spur via planer + drill + shaper), cylinders and shafts (lathe, bore). The machine cutter as a
   forged-and-tempered consumable; the hand-assembled bootstrap gear.
4. **The pass-through family** — the mill's `i1` cells so one wide roll set covers every wide gap; the
   bending roller is designed against the same mechanic but stays parked (nothing in iiex consumes a bend
   until rolled pipe).
5. Gate: a survival player rolls a roll set on the lathe, fits it, rolls a bar, crops it, and raises a
   Bessemer vessel from cast pipe — the U8 gate and B19/B23 closed together.

### Phase 3 — steam: the hammer, then the polished art *(L)*

- **3a, the steam hammer** — the owner's shape, LP steam, docked anvil, die sets, shingled slabs (six
  balls) and stamping (boilerplate into plates). With the flatwide roller from Phase 2 this is what makes
  the thesis demonstrable: a cast or shingled slab, a few feeds, a few stamps, fifteen plates.
- **3b, re-export what exists** — boiler normal-plate variant (4 atm) with its recipe, gauge frame-pinning
  and the safety-valve telegraphs; Watt engine on the grown footprint with the moved steam port and bare
  RCC paths, the planetary crank as its own block, pump and blower on the new shapes; B20 and B21 fixed
  in passing; cast valve and pressure valve on the redrawn shapes; manual pump at 1 × 2 × 2.
- **3c, the new blocks** — the exhaust-steam flow-through model (engines take steam in and hand it out at
  back-pressure; torque is the differential); horizontal engine with the integrated crank at MP height
  and chaining along x; jet condenser (two-boiler rating, vacuum back-pressure, hot-well renderer, the
  three-way condenser retired); injector; indicator blocks (three clips; the legibility question below);
  MP pump and blower on their wooden and cast tiers.
- Gate: the CB10 boiler list plus a plant of one boiler, a Watt, a horizontal engine and a condenser
  running two machines, walked in game.

### Phase 4 — the `iiex` release candidate *(M)*

Handbook pages for every station added since page 12 (machining, hammer, condenser, injector,
indicator); the deferred recipe pass (dead ingredients, creative-only items, the cupola cost row, the
orphan diagrams, cast-part consumers); `ru`/`uk`; the extensibility wiki page; the modicon; changelogs;
the released-codes manifest; the 9-target gate; the ModDB listings. Whether `siex 0.9.9` ships beside it
is question Q4.

### Phase 5 — `siex` *(L, its own roadmap)*

First the design closure, as one owner session: the gas system's four gaps and the producer, the open
hearth, the ladle and D4, alloying and hadfield, N1, the cowper remake, the Bessemer layout rebuild. Then
the build in loop order — producer gas → open hearth → ladle → ferroalloys and hadfield → HP (rolled pipe
recipes B5, rolled pressure valve B6 on the drawn art, the Cornish engine redesign, HP hammer, bearings,
Corliss). Nothing here is scheduled until Phase 4 ships.

### Parked, by name

`heavyplate` metal axis · B25 migrations · § A backport (58 rows; a fix-day, not a phase) · hardening
F1.4, F4, F5, F6.1, F7, F8 · the section class · fluid tank · ore crusher · wide hall (four mills; nothing
