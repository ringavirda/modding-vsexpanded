## The goal, restated

Two content mods on one framework - `iiex` and `siex` on `exlib` - that put a 19th-century ferrous
industry into Vintage Story as something the player *runs*: ore into a blast furnace, pig into castings
and puddled bars, bars through a rolling mill and a machine shop into the parts of a steam plant, then
steel, alloys and high pressure on top of it. The pillars are fixed in [overview.md](../../design/overview.md):
build complexity buys operating efficiency; every upgrade removes a named friction; nothing is hidden;
historically grounded; extensible by other modders. `iiex` alone must be a complete experience (the
low-tech stop), a playtest group has been waiting for it since 2026-07-30, and the release target
([scope.md](../../design/scope.md)) is the whole ferrous line.

Two consequences drive the order below:

1. **A walkable `iiex` beats breadth.** One more designed machine adds nothing a playtester can touch;
   one craftable roll set closes a loop. Everything siex-only queues behind the iiex survival loop.
2. **Footprint-changing art lands before the first release.** `iiex`/`siex` are new mods, so nothing
   published is at risk today; the moment they ship, a grown engine footprint or a moved port breaks
   somebody's save. The polished steam art therefore goes in before the release candidate, not after.

## Assessment

**What is right and should not be touched.** The thesis is coherent and rare - the whole tree is
denominated in one currency, operating friction, and every machine either creates it or buys it down.
The furnace models (heat balance, raceway carbon, breach and choke as opposites, blow-in as a stored
bit), the pipe and molten networks, the rolling two-round model with friction-walked gaps, the die and
roll-set idiom where tooling carries its job, and the gear-count spine of the machining line all reinforce
each other rather than compete. The verification culture is unusual for a mod: a parity oracle, mutation-
checked guards, and a harness that stands machines on real footprints. The August art pass raised the bar
again - sourced proportions, vanilla's own gauge vocabulary, modelled bores and readouts animated from
state - and it was done by the owner, so it is not going to be argued back down.

**Where the risk is.**

1. **The feel is unverified.** Everything from U4.4 to the boiler megablock has passed its tests and been
   seen by nobody. The mod's whole value is what those verbs feel like, and pacing numbers (stroke
   cooldowns, soak rates, coke cycle, bridge torque) are all marked "tune in play". Cheapest
   highest-value work in the repo; it is the owner's time in the client, not code.
2. **The survival loop does not close.** Roll sets are lathe-turned by ruling and there is no lathe; the
   cast pipe segments have no recipe. Both close on the machining line, which by the owner's reading is
   mostly built already (one blocktype with shape, cells and jobs by variant; hold-to-operate, the shared
   window, the footprints, the exports and the job tables remain).
3. **Breadth is the standing temptation.** Forty-five machine pages, twenty designed and unbuilt. The
   plan adds no machine a loop does not need and parks the rest by name.
4. **The steam art overhaul is a lot of code.** About twelve blocks, several new, a footprint change on a
   live engine, and a ruled model change (engines become flow-through). It is the owner's freshest
   investment and must land before the first release, so it gets its own phase.
5. **Two legibility decisions to confirm before they harden.** Indicator blocks taking readouts off plain
   pipes (Q6, open), and station windows on the machine tools - hold-to-operate ships with them, not after.
6. **`siex`'s design debt is real but not release-critical for `iiex`.** Phase 5's first task.

### Phase 3 - steam *(hammer first, then the art)*

18. **The static ladle** (owner ruling 2026-09-04: iiex, in the release). It is the only practical way
    to merge molten lines and to drop a molten flow to a lower level, since canals have no inclined
    runners. Its art is drawn (`molten-megablock-laddle.json`, 282 cubes, idle and two pour clips; the
    `molten-block-laddlecore.json` the plan also names has never been in git, so the core block needs a
    shape or a plain brick look) and its layout is specified - the unit is **U11** of the expansion
    plan (U11.2-U11.7: export, core block, the 5 x 5 x 4 multiblock with optional canal sockets, the
    3 x 3 x 3 RCC megablock lined with tier-2 refractory, recipe and cost row, docs). U11.1, filler cells
    with their own collision boxes, is what the shear's slab cells built. Scope: stands, holds one bath,
    pulls from its canals, tilts to pour into a canal start, a mold pedestal or the long cell. Not in
    this unit: alloy windows, ferroalloys, resolve-on-pour - siex extends it in Phase 5. A leaf: it can
    run in any gap the machining line leaves.
19. **The steam hammer** on the owner's shape: the machines.txt footprint, LP steam on the west port,

21. **Boiler follow-through**: the normal-plate variant as a 4 atm block with its recipe; the water-level

23. **Fittings and small pumps**: the redrawn cast valve and pressure valve swapped in; the manual pump

24. **The exhaust-steam model**: engines take steam at network pressure and hand the same litres out at

25. **Horizontal engine**: mpenergy producer with the crank at MP height, chaining along x, steam on the
