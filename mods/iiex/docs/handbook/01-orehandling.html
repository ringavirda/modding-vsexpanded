The furnace does not eat ore. It eats burden - crushed iron ore blended with lime flux - and it eats coke, and the two go in as separate layers. Nothing is premixed. The coke layers are what the blast breathes through; a shaft packed with one homogeneous material chokes.

The Burdenmaker is where burden is made. It is a brick stock house - two hoppers over one shared basin, with a sliding gate between them - and it needs no power at all, so you can build it before you own an axle. The wide hopper takes crushed iron ore; the narrow one takes lime. rightmouse adds one, ctrl + rightmouse adds a stack, and an empty hand takes it back out. Nothing is locked and nothing is spent until you say so.

Open the gate - the centre cell at the base, on the open side - and the single lid draws back, dropping both hoppers into the basin at once as one batch of burden. Reach into the basin to take it out. The basin must be empty before the next batch; that refusal is the only batch boundary this machine has, and it is what keeps two loads from averaging into each other.

Flux is burden's only quality. Block-info shows what is in each hopper and what the pair would make, named under-fluxed, standard or over-fluxed - about one part lime in twenty is standard. The grade is stamped onto the burden that comes out and travels with it, so the furnace can tell you what it is being fed.

Blocks required:
- 1 x Burdenmaker (any burned-brick colour)
- 1 x Tall Hopper, over the furnace shaft

Charging is done in rounds. The tall hopper holds one material at a time, and that is deliberate: one load lays one band. Fill it with coke and let it drip, then fill it with burden. Coke under burden, round after round, until the shaft is full. Block-info on the furnace names the course you are working on and tells you whether the charge as laid will melt or chill.

A furnace lights on a complete round, not on a quantity. The blast has to have something in front of every tuyere, so what the fire waits for is a full bottom course - charge across the whole shaft, not a heap piled down one side.

How you charge decides how hard the furnace is to blow. The blast has to get through the whole column. A coke-lean charge packs dense and demands far more pressure than a hand-driven blower can raise; a coke-rich one blows easily and eats fuel. At the iron tier you charge generously and accept the fuel cost - running lean is what the steam tier is for.

Charging a furnace that is already burning. A new layer with coke in it catches and joins the fire. Burden on its own does not - it sits there cold on top of a hot furnace, plainly unlit. Lay whole rounds and the question never comes up.

Coke, not coal. Coke is baked from coal in a coke oven; raw coal is not fuel here at all. Charcoal is accepted and carries half the carbon of coke, so a shaft charged with it runs cooler for the same layers. Crush iron ore in a pulverizer - even scrap iron bits crush back into crushed iron and go round again.

Nothing is destroyed. Break the burdenmaker and both hoppers, the basin and the brick all come back.
