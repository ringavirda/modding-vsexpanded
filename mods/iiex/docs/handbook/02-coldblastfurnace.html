The Cold Blast Furnace is the iron tier's primary smelter: a tall open-topped stack of refractory brick that reduces ore burden to molten pig iron. It smelts ore and only ore - it will not take scrap. For that you want the cupola.

Building it. Place the Cold Blast Furnace Core first - it is the bottom-centre anchor the whole build hangs off - then ctrl + shift + rightmouse it to see the build outline, and fill in every highlighted block until it reports complete.

Blocks required:
- Refractory Bricks (any tier; the core takes on the tier you build it from)
- 1 x Cold Blast Furnace Core (the anchor, placed first)
- 2 x Tuyere, low on the shaft
- 1 x Iron Tap, low in the hearth wall, and 1 x Slag Tap, higher in the same wall
- 1 x Tall Hopper, over the shaft
The shaft inside is left hollow. That is where the burden goes.

There is no door. The open top of the stack is its chimney - the furnace vents straight up and recycles nothing, which is exactly what makes it the cold blast furnace. A finished furnace is cleared by breaking its walls. Do not stand on the charge: a player who falls into a working furnace does not come out.

Blowing it. The two tuyeres are pipe nodes - run plated pipe from a Twin-Tub Blower to them and the blower will fill the line. How much pressure the furnace needs depends on the charge: a coke-rich burden blows through easily, a lean one packs dense and needs more than a mechanical blower can raise. If the line will not hold pressure, check that the run is sealed - an open pipe end vents to atmosphere.

Firing it. Let the hopper fill the shaft, ignite the burden, and the furnace climbs on its own. It has no fixed maximum temperature: what it reaches is a balance between the heat the coke and the blast put in, and what the stack radiates away and the cold charge soaks up. A rich burden on cold blast settles comfortably past iron's 1482 C melting line. A lean one stalls short of it, and no amount of patience will help - it needs preheated blast, which needs the steam tier.

Block-info always shows the current temperature, the threshold it is trying to clear, and both sides of the ledger, so a stall reads as a number and not as a mystery.

Tapping it. Once it is Melting, molten pig collects behind the iron tap and slag behind the slag tap above it. Open a tap and the metal runs out. With nothing plumbed to it, it solidifies at the tap and is chiselled free by hand; with a molten canal under it, it runs off to be cast properly.

The fire is fragile. Running out of burden, choking the tuyeres, or letting the blast die all count against the furnace, and enough of it puts the fire out.
