Coke is the fuel the whole iron tier runs on. It is the fuel half of every blast furnace and cupola charge, and it is what burns in the fireboxes of the puddling and reheating furnaces. You can already make it a little at a time, one sealed chamber of coal per bake; the Coke Oven is the same process at the scale a working ironworks needs.

It is a bank of two chambers sharing one wall, built of ordinary fire brick - the same masonry a small chamber wants, and nothing more expensive, because everything downstream waits on this one building. Each chamber holds six cells of coal against a small chamber's single pile, and each is closed by two things: a charge lid in the crown above it and a charge door at the front.

Charge it through an open door - rightmouse with coal in hand against a chamber and the whole bed fills at once. It takes bituminous coal and nothing else: charcoal and coke are not coal, anthracite will not coke at all, and lignite is too poor to be worth the chamber. Finding a bituminous seam is therefore part of getting into iron, not a detail.

Then shut it. Coking is destructive distillation - the coal has to be heated out of contact with air, driving off its volatiles and leaving almost pure carbon behind - so a chamber standing open bakes nothing at all, however long you leave it. Shut the lid and the door, light the oven, and read the block for each chamber's progress; a chamber you have left open says so rather than sitting there quietly doing nothing.

The two chambers keep their own clocks. You can draw one side and charge it again while the other is still working, which is the point of building a bank rather than a single chamber, and opening one chamber's lid does not interrupt the other. Opening a lid mid-bake only pauses that chamber; shut it again and it carries on from where it stopped.

What comes out is ordinary coke, rather more of it per unit of coal than a small chamber returns - the arched crown holds the heat where the charge is instead of losing it. Take it out through the drawing door and lay it into a blast furnace charge, a cupola, or a firebox.
